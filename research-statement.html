Research Statement

Devis Wawan Saputra · AI in Education, learning analytics, and learning systems

Research focus

My research examines how educational data and machine learning can support the design and evaluation of learning systems without overstating what the evidence shows. I am particularly interested in knowledge tracing, early-course assessment signals, data provenance, model evaluation, and responsible use of AI in educational decisions.

Current empirical work

Two 2026 working manuscripts form the core of my current research portfolio. The knowledge-tracing study examines how differences in ASSISTments 2009 source data and available learner history affect model comparisons, including evidence drawn from ASSISTments 2015. The OULAD day-30 analysis investigates early assessment submission and subsequent outcomes using explicit weighting and overlap diagnostics. The observational design does not establish a causal effect of submission timing.

These manuscripts should be assessed as working research rather than peer-reviewed journal articles. Current public availability and arXiv identifiers are tracked separately on the publications page.

Methodological commitments

  • Data provenance: Track dataset versions, corrections, preprocessing decisions, and the consequences of source variation.
  • Fair comparisons: Define baselines, information budgets, and outcome metrics before interpreting model rankings.
  • Uncertainty and limitations: Report overlap, imbalance, evaluation constraints, and threats to generalization rather than converting association into causal claims.
  • Reproducibility: Keep manuscript, code, and reported metrics synchronized, while distinguishing independently verified results from unverified claims.

Experience informing this direction

My background combines informatics, university teaching, engineering-management program leadership, curriculum design, and workplace learning-system implementation. These experiences inform the practical questions I ask about learner data, educational evaluation, and the design of systems that educators can inspect and use responsibly. They do not substitute for empirical validation of new AI methods.

Open research questions

I am interested in investigating the robustness of educational prediction under realistic data constraints, the reproducibility of knowledge-tracing comparisons, and ways to make AI-supported educational assessments more transparent and useful to instructors. These are research directions, not claims of completed or validated interventions.
