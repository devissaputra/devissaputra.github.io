Empirical Study · AI in Education

Knowledge Tracing Benchmark on ASSISTments 2009

This study asks how much predictive value different forms of learner history add when the evaluation is kept genuinely learner-disjoint. It uses 4,148 ASSISTments 2009 learners and 274,331 interactions, splitting learners rather than individual attempts into 70% training, 15% validation, and 15% final test partitions. Under one frozen protocol, it compares a global prior, a skill prior, fixed and validation-tuned Bayesian Knowledge Tracing, a PFA-style logistic model based on prior learner-skill successes and failures, and a compact GRU sequence model. Every stateful prediction is generated before the response being predicted is observed, preventing the current answer from leaking into the model state.

On the 623-learner final test set, containing 41,228 interactions, the compact GRU produced ROC-AUC 0.7470, Brier 0.1800, log loss 0.5382, and ECE-10 0.0133 at seed 42. Across the prespecified seeds 13, 42, and 73, its ROC-AUC remained 0.7471 ± 0.0008 and Brier score 0.1798 ± 0.0002. The benchmark also separates first-seen from repeated skill encounters, bootstraps Brier-score differences by learner rather than by interaction, and inspects high-error skills. These are predictive results on a historical tutoring dataset, not direct measurements of student knowledge, teaching effectiveness, or readiness for high-stakes educational decisions.

Empirical Study · AI in Education

Causal Learning Analytics — OULAD Day-30 Landmark Study

This study asks a deliberately narrow observational question: among learners still eligible at day 30, what adjusted contrast in favorable final outcome is associated with submitting at least one non-banked assessment by that landmark? The design separates eligibility, exposure, and later outcome timing so the analysis does not quietly mix early withdrawal with treatment assignment.

Data source: Open University Learning Analytics Dataset (OULAD), using a frozen day-30 landmark cohort.

Empirical Study · AI in Education

Misconception-Aware RAG Tutor — Empirical Study

This repository separates an empirical retrieval study from the tutoring prototype built around it. The study tests whether conditioning evidence retrieval on an observed wrong answer changes support-passage retrieval on SciQ, while a negative-control condition checks whether any gain is more than the effect of simply appending extra distractor-like text.

Data source: SciQ test split from the Allen Institute for AI / Hugging Face dataset repository, pinned to a frozen revision and hash.

Empirical Study · AI in Education

Responsible AIED Evaluation — UCI 697 Study

This bundle audits an enrollment-time dropout probability model across discrimination, calibration, subgroup error patterns, allocation behavior, target definitions, and split sensitivity. Protected-group fields remain audit-only, semester-performance variables are removed to respect the prediction-time boundary, and the results are framed as measurement evidence rather than a fairness certificate.

Data source: UCI Predict Students' Dropout and Academic Success (dataset 697), DOI 10.24432/C5MC89.

GenAI Learning Observatory

An observability layer for studying how learners use generative AI rather than merely counting usage. It keeps prompting, verification, revision, reflection, and answer adoption as separate signals so learner-agency assumptions can be inspected instead of disappearing inside a single engagement score.

Teacher–AI Assessment Studio

A human-in-the-loop assessment prototype that separates AI rubric scores, teacher scores, disagreement, uncertainty, routing, and final resolution. The design treats automated scoring as decision support and makes the path to a final judgment reconstructable for audit and research.

Privacy-Preserving Learning Analytics

A federated-learning research prototype in which synthetic learner records stay in local client shards while gradients are aggregated centrally. Gradient clipping and Gaussian noise can be varied to study utility sensitivity, while the repository is explicit that this is not a formal differential-privacy guarantee without privacy accounting and a threat model.

Multimodal Self-Regulation Lab

A multimodal sandbox that keeps interaction, gaze-like attention, self-report, missing modalities, reliability estimation, fusion, and ablation visible. The goal is to study when another modality adds useful information, when it adds noise, and how reliability-aware fusion can remain interpretable.

Learning Design Process Mining

A process-mining view of instructional design that treats design work as an event log rather than only a finished artifact. It measures trace variants, transitions, rework, iteration, bottlenecks, and human–AI handoffs so the design process itself becomes analyzable.

Hybrid Intelligence Lab

An experimental harness for testing when a human, an AI system, or a shared process should decide or defer. Human performance, model confidence, disagreement, routing, override, and deferral remain separate so any gain from hybrid decision-making can be traced to concrete cases.

A compact lab for testing whether educational AI explanations actually track model behavior. Comprehensiveness, sufficiency, stability, and perturbation-based checks are used to distinguish explanations that sound plausible from explanations that remain faithful to the prediction process.

A behavioral-proxy framework for studying possible cognitive offloading during AI-assisted learning.
